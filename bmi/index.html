Optional: check waist circumference
For Indian adults, waist circumference of 80 cm or more in women and 90 cm or more in men is a commonly used central obesity threshold.

All values are approximate. The range from BMI 18.5 to below 23 is the Indian adult reference range. BMI 23 marks the start of overweight and BMI 25 marks the start of obesity; neither number is an individually prescribed target.

How to read the result

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (metres)]². For example, 65 kg at 160 cm gives BMI 25.4. BMI cannot distinguish muscle from fat, and waist measurement, medical history and other factors may change clinical interpretation. The result is for adults 18 years and older, not children.

Clinical reference & sources

Indian consensus categories: underweight below 18.5; reference range 18.5 to below 23; overweight 23 to below 25; obesity at 25 or above. The original 2009 Asian Indian consensus and an Indian government clinical guidance document support these thresholds.

  • Misra and colleagues, Indian consensus statement (2009)
  • Government of India clinical guidance: Indian BMI and waist cutoffs

Clinical interpretation: Do not prescribe weight loss during pregnancy from this calculator. Review nutritional intake, maternal condition and fetal growth; investigate unexpected weight loss or rapid gain. The 5–9 kg Indian limited-gain statement is not a mandatory 5 kg minimum. An early-pregnancy weight entered as a proxy makes all prepregnancy-based results approximate.
Guidelines, limitations and source links

ICMR–NIN 2024 gives 10–12 kg for normal BMI at conception (18.5 to 23), closer follow-up without a numerical total for BMI below 18.5, and a limited gain of no more than approximately 5–9 kg for overweight (23–27.5) and obese (>27.5). This is a total-pregnancy guide, not a validated week-by-week Indian curve.

The optional 2009 IOM ranges use different prepregnancy BMI categories: below 18.5, 18.5–24.9, 25–29.9 and 30 or more. Its second-/third-trimester rates assume roughly 0.5–2 kg first-trimester gain. The week-based band here is arithmetic illustration only, not a clinical centile. These ranges apply to singleton pregnancies.
